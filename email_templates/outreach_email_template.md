# Outreach email template — website offer for trades and contractors

DEMO CONTENT. "Tradie Web Co" is a fictional studio and
`tradiewebco.example` is a reserved, non-routable domain. Swap the sign-off
for your own details before this is used on real recipients.

Use this template for every prospect. Same email, two substitutions.

Subject:
```text
A website for your trade business?
```

Only change:
- the name in the greeting
- the single observation line between the first and third paragraphs

Greeting rule:
- Use a person's first name if it can be inferred confidently.
- Otherwise use `Hello,`
- Do not use `Company name + team` because it can sound automated.

```text
Hello [Name],

I'm Alex Morgan from Tradie Web Co. We build websites for trade and contracting
businesses — plumbers, sparkies, builders, roofers, landscapers — so customers
can find you, see the work you've done, and get a quote request in front of you
without playing phone tag.

[Observation line]

A typical site takes about two weeks and covers the services you offer, a photo
gallery of finished jobs, your reviews, your service area, and a quote form that
lands straight in your inbox. We sort the domain, the hosting and your Google
listing, and it looks right on a phone, because that's where your customers will
open it.

If you'd like to see what yours would look like, just reply to this email and
I'll put together a free one-page mock-up for you — no charge, no obligation.

Thanks,
Alex Morgan
Tradie Web Co
https://www.tradiewebco.example
```

## Writing the observation line

One or two sentences, grounded in something you actually saw. It is the only
part of the email that proves a human-shaped process looked at this business.

Pick whichever fits what you found:

- **No website at all** — they trade off a Facebook page or a directory listing.
  > I couldn't find a website for [Business] — you're listed on [where], and
  > that's doing a lot of work on its own.
- **Site exists but is dated or broken on mobile** — say what you saw, plainly.
  > Your site's got everything on it, but it squashes up a bit on a phone, and
  > that's where most quote enquiries start these days.
- **Site exists and is fine** — no website pitch. Skip the prospect.

Never claim a site is "unprofessional", "losing you money", or "invisible on
Google" — you cannot know that, and it reads as a shakedown. Describe what is
there; let them draw the conclusion.
